# Sourdough Brioche Cinnamon Rolls

🔗 **Source:** Central Milling (recipe by Nicky Giusto)

- 🍽️ **Yield:** about 10 to 11 rolls
- 🥖 **Flour:** Organic "Type 80" Old-World Bread Flour
- 🌡️ **Dough temperature:** 75 to 80°F
- ⏳ **Levain fermentation:** 8 to 12 hours at 70 to 75°F
- ⏳ **First fermentation:** 1 hour at room temperature (70°F)
- ❄️ **Second fermentation:** 12 to 16 hours in the refrigerator (40°F); the source's technical table and shaping step say up to 18 hours
- ⏲️ **Proofing:** 90 to 120 minutes at room temperature
- 🔥 **Oven:** 350°F (175°C)
- ⏱️ **Bake:** 18 to 20 minutes (the author baked about 22 minutes for more color)

The weights below are grams, with baker's percentages in parentheses, as given in the source.

---

## 🛒 Ingredients

### Levain

- 🌾 89 g organic "Type 80" Old-World Bread Flour (100%)
- 💧 71 g water (80%)
- 🦠 18 g sourdough (20%)
- ⚖️ Total: 178 g (200%)

### Final dough

- 🌾 356 g organic "Type 80" Old-World Bread Flour (100%)
- 🧂 8 g sea salt (2.3%)
- 🦠 8 g instant yeast (2.3%)
- 🥛 18 g milk (5%)
- 🥚 237 g whole egg (70%)
- 🍬 71 g granulated sugar (20%)
- 🧈 125 g butter, tempered (35%)
- 🥣 178 g levain (35%)
- ⚖️ Total: 1000 g (281%)

### Filling

- 🍬 100 g brown sugar (100%)
- 🍬 100 g granulated sugar (100%)
- 🌰 80 g toasted pine nuts (80%)
- 🍇 100 g golden raisins (or any raisin) (100%)
- 🍂 5 g cinnamon (3%)
- 🍂 5 g cardamom powder (3%)
- 🍊 Zest of one orange (weight not given)
- ⚖️ Total: 390 g (390%, not counting the orange zest)

### Icing

- ❄️ 100 g powdered sugar (100%)
- 💧 14 g warm water (14%)
- 🍋 3 g lemon juice (3%)
- ⚖️ Total: 117 g (117%)

## 👩‍🍳 Steps

1. 🥣 The morning before you want to bake the rolls, prepare your levain. Watch the ambient temperature to be sure it matures properly.
2. 🥚 The evening before, or about 12 to 18 hours before you want to eat the rolls, scale the ingredients. Add all the levain, egg (straight from the refrigerator), milk, flour, salt, and instant dry yeast to the mixing bowl. Scale the sugar and butter separately; you will add them at the end of the mix.
3. 🥖 Mix the levain, egg, milk, flour, salt, and instant dry yeast until it is well developed. The source says this normally takes about 7 to 9 minutes in a 6-quart KitchenAid on speed 4 to 7.
4. 🧈 Once the dough is pulling away from the sides of the bowl and looks smooth and a little glossy, add the sugar and butter. Mix on speed 2 to 5 until completely incorporated. Some dough sticking to the sides of the bowl is okay.
5. 🤲 Remove the dough from the bowl with a plastic scraper. With a little water or oil on your hands, tighten the dough into a round. Cover with plastic or a kitchen towel and let it rest for one hour.
6. 🍂 While the dough rests, prepare the filling for the next day. Combine the sugars, nuts, spices, and orange zest. You can add the raisins here too, or separate the raisins and zest, add the juice of half the orange, and let this sit in the refrigerator overnight.
7. 🧊 Transfer the dough ball to a quarter sheet pan and lightly push the dough out to a rough rectangle. Cover and let it rest in the refrigerator overnight, up to 18 hours.
8. 🧴 The morning you are ready to bake, prepare your pans or baking vessels. Spray them with a non-stick spray or brush the interior of the pans with melted butter. Transfer the cold dough to a lightly dusted counter. Using a rolling pin, roll the dough into a 16 x 16-inch square.
9. 💧 Brush the dough with a light coating of water. Then evenly distribute the filling, leaving about an inch at the top (so the dough will seal once you roll it up).
10. 🌀 Roll it up. Then use a serrated knife to cut sections about 1.5 to 1.75 inches thick. As you cut the sections, transfer the rolls to your baking pan. The author uses a 10-inch cake pan; any pan can be used.
11. 🥖 You should have about 10 to 11 rolls. If you have a few extra, place them in any oven-safe baking vessel.
12. ⏲️ Proof the rolls at room temperature for about 2 hours. With about 30 to 45 minutes remaining in the proof time, preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C).
13. 🔥 Once proofed, bake the rolls for about 18 to 20 minutes. Bake time will vary depending on your oven. The author baked these for about 22 minutes for more color; for softer, lighter rolls, take them out around the 18- to 19-minute mark.
14. ❄️ Let the rolls cool for about an hour. You can drizzle the icing on the rolls after they are cool, or wait until you serve them.

## 📝 Notes

- 🌟 You will need an active sourdough starter on hand, and you need to start the process about 24 hours before you want to enjoy the rolls.
- 🌟 Source: Central Milling, "Sourdough Brioche Cinnamon Rolls," recipe by Nicky Giusto, as stated in the source text.
- 🌟 Filling nuts: you don't have to use pine nuts. Any nut can be used if it is chopped to the size of pine nuts. Sliced almonds are a suggested alternative.
- 🌟 The source's technical notes give mixing times for a KitchenAid: speed 3 to 4 for 5 minutes, then speed 6 to 7 for 3 to 5 minutes.
- 🌟 Mixing times can vary depending on the mixer. Baking time and temperature can vary depending on the oven.
- 🌟 The source gives the liquid temperature as 38 to 43°F for refrigerated eggs.
- 🌟 Once the dough comes out of the refrigerator, the source says to work quickly and efficiently, since the dough is much easier to handle cold. Read ahead and have everything prepared.
- 🌟 The source does not give the method for mixing the icing beyond its ingredients and says to drizzle it on the rolls.
