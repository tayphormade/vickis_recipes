# Mardi Gras King Cake

🔗 **Source:** [Back to the Cutting Board](http://backtothecuttingboard.com/dessert/mardi-gras-king-cake/)

- 🍽️ **Servings:** 1 very large or 2 smaller king cakes
- ⏲️ **Prep:** 3 hours
- 🔥 **Bake:** 25-30 minutes at 375°F (191°C), until golden brown (the author needed only 20 minutes)
- ⏱️ **Total:** 3 hours 30 minutes

---

## 🛒 Ingredients

### Cake

- 💧 1/2 cup warm water, about 105 to 115 degrees
- 🍞 2 envelopes active dry yeast
- 🌾 4 1/2 to 5 1/2 cups sifted flour (the author started with 4 1/2 and added more)
- 🍬 1/2 cup sugar
- 🌰 1/2 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
- 🧂 2 tsp salt
- 🍋 1 tsp grated lemon zest
- 🥛 1/2 cup lukewarm milk
- 🥚 3 eggs
- 🥚 4 egg yolks
- 🧈 1/2 cup butter, softened
- 🧈 2 tbsp butter
- 🥚 1 egg, lightly beaten (for brushing)
- 🥛 1 tbsp milk (for brushing)
- 🎁 A tiny doll or coin (optional)

### Icing

- 🍬 3 cups confectioner's sugar
- 🍋 1/4 cup lemon juice, strained
- 💧 3 to 5 tbsp water

### Sugar topping

- 🎨 Food coloring: purple (you can do two drops of red, one of blue), green, and yellow
- 🍬 3/4 cup sugar

---

## 👩‍🍳 Steps

1. 🍞 Combine the yeast and the warm water.
2. 🥣 Combine the flour, sugar, nutmeg, and salt in a large mixing bowl. Stir in the lemon peel. Make a well in the center and add the yeast and water mixture, along with the milk. Lightly beat the 3 eggs and 4 egg yolks, and add them to the liquid mixture. With a large wooden spoon, gradually incorporate the dry ingredients into the liquids in the center well.
3. 🧈 Beat in 1/2 cup of the butter and continue beating until the dough forms a ball (the author needed to add about 1/2 to 3/4 cup of flour to get the dough to form a ball). Use a food processor or dough hook for beating, if desired.
4. 🤲 Place the ball on a floured surface and knead until smooth and elastic, incorporating more flour as necessary, a little at a time.
5. 🧈 Butter the inside of a large bowl with 1 tablespoon of the butter. Place the dough in the bowl and turn so the entire surface is buttered. Cover the bowl and set aside for 1 to 1 1/2 hours, or until doubled in bulk.
6. 🧈 Brush a large baking sheet with the remaining butter. Punch the dough down on a lightly floured surface. Knead, then pat and shape the dough into a cylinder about 14 inches long. Alternately, you can divide the dough into three 14-inch-long cylinders and braid them together. Place on the baking sheet and form into a ring. Press the coin or doll well into the dough so it doesn't show. Set aside to rise for about 45 to 60 minutes.
7. 🔥 When ready to bake, beat together one egg and 1 tbsp of milk, and brush the top and sides of the ring. Bake in the middle of the oven at 375°F (191°C) for 25 to 30 minutes, or until golden brown (the author only needed 20 minutes). Slide the cake onto a wire rack to cool.
8. 🍬 Icing: combine the confectioners' sugar, lemon juice, and 3 tablespoons of water in a deep bowl; stir until smooth. If too stiff to spread, beat in 1 teaspoon of water at a time until the desired consistency is reached. It should be thin enough to run slowly down the sides of the cake. Spread the icing over the top of the cake, letting it run down the sides.
9. 🌈 Sprinkle the colored sugars over the icing immediately, forming a row of purple, yellow, and green strips on both sides of the ring.

## 📝 Notes

- 📖 The source calls this a "King Cake from About.com." It is posted on Back to the Cutting Board (Emily Carlin, February 23, 2009).
- 🎨 The author made colored sugar at home: fill a plastic bag with about 1/4 cup of sugar, add 1-3 drops of food coloring (depending on how dark you want it), seal the bag, and rub the sugar between your hands until the coloring coats all of it.
- 🥖 The dough got very big. The author baked it on a 14x16 baking sheet and said that next time she would divide the dough and make two smaller cakes.
- 🧶 Braiding is harder, and the author's braid was not very good. A plain oval is easier, but the author thinks a braid looks more interesting.
- 🥧 The author notes that this cake is more like a cinnamon roll than a cake. Some versions contain cinnamon, but this one has only nutmeg. Filled versions with cream cheese, chocolate, lemon, apple, or cherry also exist. The author also wrote a later update about a filled version with lemon and cream cheese.
