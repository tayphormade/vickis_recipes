# Italian Fig Cookies (Cuccidati)

- 🍽️ **Yield:** 75 cookies
- 🕑 **Total time:** 2 hours 3 minutes
- 🌡️ **Oven:** 350°F (175°C)
- ⏲️ **Bake:** 15 to 18 minutes

---

## 🛒 Ingredients

### Fig filling

- 🍑 12 oz dried figs (about 20 whole figs, stems removed)
- 🍇 ¾ c raisins (check for stems)
- 🌰 ¾ c walnuts
- 🍯 ⅓ c apricot preserves
- 🌿 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- 🥄 ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
- 🍊 Zest of 1 medium orange

### Dough

- 🌾 3½ c all-purpose flour
- 🧂 ½ tsp salt
- 🧪 1 tsp baking powder
- 🍬 ¾ c granulated sugar
- 🧈 1 c cold butter
- 🥚 2 eggs
- 🌼 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 💧 ½ – ¾ c water

### Icing

- ❄️ 1 c powdered sugar / confectioners sugar
- 🥛 1 Tbsp milk
- 🌈 Rainbow nonpareil sprinkles

---

## 👩‍🍳 Steps

1. 🥣 Filling: in the bowl of a food processor, add all of the filling ingredients. Pulse until a smooth, thick paste forms. All ingredients should be well blended, with no large pieces of fruit or nuts.
2. 🫙 Cover the filling and set it aside.
3. 🥣 Dough: in a large bowl, add the flour, salt, baking powder, and sugar. Whisk together until combined.
4. 🧈 Using a box grater, shred the cold butter over a small piece of parchment paper. Add the butter to the bowl of dry ingredients and gently toss until coated.
5. 🥚 In a small bowl, beat the eggs slightly with a fork. Add the vanilla extract and stir. Pour the egg mixture into the flour and butter mixture.
6. 💧 Add the water, starting with ½ cup. Gently stir the wet ingredients into the flour (a Danish whisk works well here). If the dough looks dry, add a little more water, but be careful not to add too much: the dough should never become wet or sticky.
7. 🤲 Using your hands, bring the dough together into a large ball.
8. 🧊 Wrap the ball in plastic wrap and let it rest in the fridge for 1 hour.
9. 🔥 Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and prepare 2 baking sheets with parchment paper.
10. 🫗 Add the fig filling to a piping bag or large zip-lock bag. Cut a ½-inch opening at the tip.
11. 🌾 Prepare a surface for rolling the dough. Take a portion of the dough and place it on a well-floured surface.
12. 🥖 Roll the dough to ⅛ inch thick in a long rectangle that is at least 5–6 inches wide.
13. 🔪 Using a sharp knife, trim the dough to approximately 5–6 inches wide and 10–12 inches long. It's OK if the dough is not as long; width is really the most important.
14. 🍑 Leaving a ½-inch edge on one side of the dough, pipe the filling down the full length of the dough area.
15. 🌀 With floured hands, use the edge to roll the dough over the piped filling until it meets the other side of the dough surface.
16. 🔪 Cut along the seam and pull the roll away from the remaining rolled dough. Rotate the cookie roll so the seam is on the bottom, and gently press to seal.
17. 📏 Cut into 1½-inch pieces.
18. 🔁 Repeat with the remaining rolled dough. For the second roll, there may be a small amount of excess dough on the edge, which should be trimmed.
19. 🍪 Place the cookies on the prepared baking sheets, leaving about 1½ inches of space between each cookie.
20. ⏲️ Bake for 15–18 minutes, or until very lightly golden brown.
21. ⏳ Let the cookies set on the tray for 5 minutes, then transfer them to a wire rack to cool completely.
22. 🥣 While the cookies cool, make the icing: in a small bowl, combine the powdered sugar and milk until completely smooth.
23. 🫗 Add the icing to a piping bag or zip-lock bag and cut a small amount off the tip. Drizzle the icing on top of the cookies without letting it drip down the sides, so the fig filling still shows.
24. 🌈 Top with colored sprinkles, such as rainbow nonpareils.

## 📝 Notes

- 📚 Source: OwlbBaking.com, "Italian Fig Cookies (Cuccidati)."
- 🧈 The source says a box grater is the easiest way to shred the butter. You can also cut the butter in by hand with a pastry blender, or use a food processor, pulsing the butter and flour until they resemble coarse crumbs.
- 🧊 The cookies can be made a couple of days ahead, but the sprinkles' color will bleed into the icing over time. For longer storage, freeze the cookies un-iced and ice them the day of.
- 🍑 Dried figs often form a sugary, crystallized outer coating. The source says it's fine and safe to eat, and can be rinsed off with water if you prefer.
- 🧰 Equipment named in the source: food processor, piping bag or large zip-lock bag, box grater.
